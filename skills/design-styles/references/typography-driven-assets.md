# Typography-Driven Images & Graphic Assets Guide 2026

> Comprehensive, brand-neutral art direction for photography, illustration, icons, borders, separators, typographic graphics, lettering, motion, and responsive media.

---

## 1. Purpose

Supporting assets must strengthen the type rather than compete with it. Photography provides context or evidence, illustration supports narrative, letterforms become graphic assets, rules create structure, motion adds emphasis, and icons remain quiet functional tools.

## 2. Asset hierarchy

Classify assets as primary typographic expression, evidence, explanation, navigation, or atmosphere. Supporting assets must not weaken reading hierarchy. Evidence and explanation outrank decorative atmosphere.

## 3. Art-direction models

Choose one primary model: type-first with minimal imagery; type and photography in equal tension; type over controlled media; type as mask; typographic collage; image sequence with captions; type-led editorial; kinetic type with static support; or hand lettering plus rational UI.

## 4. Photography role

Photography should provide evidence, context, emotional contrast, human presence, product/place information, a surface for composition, or a controlled source of color and texture. Do not add generic imagery simply because the page seems empty.

## 5. Photography composition

Plan text-safe area, subject position, crop direction, image contrast, negative space, expected headline size, mobile crop, and fallback. Align type to environmental or architectural lines, use defined grid fields, and create relationships rather than arbitrary overlay.

## 6. Text over photography

Use solid panels, local scrims, controlled gradients, predictable light/dark zones, or separate text on mobile. Do not rely on one dark region surviving every crop, and do not place body copy over moving video.

## 7. Image treatment

Natural editorial, monochrome, limited duotone, high-contrast graphic crops, restrained grain, direct flash, flat studio color, and documentary natural light are suitable. Match treatment to the type character and avoid several competing treatments.

## 8. Ratios and captions

Define a limited ratio system and mobile-specific crops. Captions use the functional type system and may include number, description, date, location, credit, and source. Required credits should remain visible rather than hover-only.

## 9. Illustration

Illustration may be geometric, editorial linework, hand-drawn, collage-based, lettering-integrated, diagrammatic, or abstracted from glyphs. Define a consistent stroke, edge, palette, perspective, and detail level. Generic vector scenes are usually inappropriate.

## 10. Lettering versus typography

Lettering is custom-drawn for a specific word; typography uses reusable fonts and rules. Use lettering for logos, campaign titles, posters, and limited identity moments. Do not use it for navigation, forms, or long reading. Provide exact accessible wording when lettering is artwork.

## 11. Typographic graphics

Oversized numerals, dates, coordinates, punctuation, letter fragments, repeated words, alphabets, quotations, and folio systems may become graphic assets. Keep meaningful wording live where possible and hide decorative repetition from assistive technology.

## 12. Type as mask and layered type

Text may mask photography, gradients, video, or texture for short display moments. Preserve recognition, provide a solid fallback, and avoid small critical text. Outline and layered type require adequate stroke and contrast; do not stack effects until glyphs become unclear.

## 13. Variable-font graphics

Weight, width, slant, optical size, or custom axes may create responsive fitting, hover emphasis, section transitions, data-led display, or campaign animation. Stay within designed axis ranges and avoid extremes that damage spacing and recognition.

## 14. Kinetic asset types

Scrolling typography moves across a plane; dynamic layout moves text elements relative to one another; fluid typography morphs glyphs; reveal typography uses masks or opacity; reactive typography responds to input or state. Every type needs a static fallback and communication purpose.

## 15. Motion production

Document purpose, trigger, duration, easing, loop, pause, reduced-motion version, static export, mobile behavior, and performance budget. Avoid long paragraph animation, moving controls, and constant movement beside reading text.

## 16. Video and type

Kinetic type may synchronize with campaign or event video, but decorative words are not captions. Provide proper captions, no autoplay audio, user pause, poster frame, static/reduced version, and essential information outside video.

## 17. Icons and arrows

Icons remain subordinate and use one grid, stroke, fill, cap, join, and optical-size system. Arrows communicate navigation, continuation, sequence, or relationship and should relate in weight to the typography. Decorative arrows pointing nowhere are prohibited.

## 18. Borders and separators

Baseline rules, underlines, overlines, brackets, corner marks, indexed dividers, glyphs, punctuation, repeated words, and color shifts may structure content. Keep weights controlled and ensure decorative repeated text does not create screen-reader noise.

## 19. Patterns and texture

Patterns may derive from glyphs, punctuation, numbers, words, baseline grids, or typographic measurements. Texture may reference print, paper, ink, photocopy, paint, or scanning. Keep both subordinate and never degrade live text.

## 20. Collage

Combine type, photography, paper, illustration, archive, handwriting, and geometry through a clear layer hierarchy: background, primary message, evidence, supporting label, annotation, texture. Preserve readable DOM order despite visual overlap.

## 21. Diagrams and data

Use stable functional typography, direct labels, limited node styles, tabular numerals, units, annotations, and accessible alternatives. Do not turn diagrams or charts into decorative type at the expense of accuracy.

## 22. Social assets

Design explicit compositions for 1:1, 4:5, 9:16, 1.91:1, and landscape displays. Do not auto-crop a desktop hero. Store exact copy as data so artwork can be regenerated without spelling errors.

## 23. Responsive delivery

Keep typography live and deliver imagery separately with picture, srcset, sizes, intrinsic dimensions, AVIF/WebP, LCP priority, and mobile crops. Do not bake the responsive headline into the image.

## 24. Accessibility

Classify assets as informative, functional, decorative, complex, or typographic artwork. Provide exact accessible wording for artwork, alt text for evidence, descriptions for complex graphics, strong overlay contrast, reduced motion, and hidden decorative repetition.

## 25. Rights and provenance

Record photographer/artist, type designer/foundry, source, date, license, modification rights, consent, archive source, AI status, approved usage, and version. Font and image licenses are separate. Never distribute proprietary fonts without permission.

## 26. AI-generated lettering

Use AI for exploration, abstract letterform ideas, typographic textures, and nonfinal mood studies. Do not rely on generated raster text for logos, dates, prices, navigation, legal wording, campaign copy, or multilingual content. Rebuild approved wording as real type or verified lettering.

## 27. File naming

Use descriptive names such as hero-cultural-program-mobile.avif, portrait-artist-direct-flash-4x5.webp, texture-photocopy-grain.webp, illustration-letterform-sequence.svg, motion-variable-width-title.webm, and poster-kinetic-title-static.webp.

## 28. Anti-patterns

Avoid generic photography behind giant type, text baked into every image, misspelled AI lettering, crops fighting headline placement, mixed icon sets, arrows without purpose, weak outline contrast, repeated text announced by screen readers, body copy treated as artwork, unreadable diagram labels, auto-cropped social assets, and unlicensed font artwork.

## 29. AI-agent instructions

Preserve typography as the primary system; classify support assets; plan media around live text; define crops, illustration, icon, arrow, rule, pattern, motion, and fallback systems; verify exact wording; separate decorative repetition from screen-reader content; record rights; avoid generated exact lettering; and create mobile/social compositions independently.

## 30. Production checklist

Verify the type-first model, text-safe photography, mobile crops, caption system, live-text versus artwork decisions, variable axes, motion pause and reduction, icon and arrow vocabulary, border/separator rules, diagram typography, data typography, exact wording access, alt text, font licenses, image rights, and AI status.

## Final rule

Supporting visuals must create context, evidence, contrast, or rhythm without replacing the type system. Exact words remain controlled, accessible, and readable; imagery supports them, motion emphasizes them, and graphics organize them.

# Research Basis v2 — Typographic Artwork, Motion, and Supporting Assets

## A. Research foundation

This guide relies on:

- WCAG requirements for images of text, contrast, resize, reflow, and text spacing.
- MDN and Google Fonts documentation for variable-font capabilities and animation.
- web.dev guidance for font and image performance.
- Current visual-trend sources only for aesthetic context.

## B. Source-to-rule mapping

### B.1 Typographic artwork and exact wording

WCAG's Images of Text criterion supports live text when web technology can achieve the presentation.

**Rules:**

- Exact campaign wording, dates, navigation, labels, prices, and legal text remain live.
- Custom lettering used as artwork requires exact accessible wording.
- Decorative repeated words should not be exposed repeatedly to screen readers.
- Social assets must store exact copy separately from the rendered image.

### B.2 Variable-font motion

MDN and Google Fonts document animatable variable axes and low-level axis control.

**Rules:**

- Validate the axis and range in the selected font.
- Do not animate an unsupported or invented axis.
- Avoid morphing that damages glyph recognition.
- Use motion for semantic emphasis or identity, not continuous distraction.
- Provide a static export and reduced-motion behavior.

### B.3 Reflow and mobile compositions

WCAG reflow and resize requirements support creating separate mobile typographic compositions rather than relying on cropped desktop posters.

**Rules:**

- Recompose line breaks for mobile.
- Do not place exact text only inside desktop artwork.
- Avoid rotated or vertical reading text on narrow screens.
- Ensure social and campaign variants are designed for each ratio.

### B.4 Font and image performance

**Rules:**

- Separate live typography from photography to avoid unnecessary raster dimensions.
- Use responsive media independent of the text layer.
- Optimize textures and motion assets.
- Avoid GIF for long kinetic sequences.
- Compare static and variable font payloads.
- Prevent font-loading layout shift.
