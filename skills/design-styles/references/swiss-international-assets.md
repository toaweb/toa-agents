# Swiss / International Typographic Style — Images & Graphic Assets Guide 2026

> A comprehensive, brand-neutral guide for photography, illustration, icons, borders, separators, diagrams, charts, maps, patterns, motion assets, and visual production in Swiss-inspired websites.
>
> Use this document together with `swiss-international-web-design-system-2026.md`.

---

## 1. Purpose

Swiss / International visual assets should support information rather than compete with it.

The visual system must help users:

- understand;
- compare;
- navigate;
- identify;
- verify;
- interpret;
- act.

Visual assets should be:

- clear;
- precise;
- relevant;
- systematic;
- scalable;
- accessible;
- accurately captioned;
- consistently produced.

---

## 2. Visual hierarchy

Classify assets into four levels.

## 2.1 Evidence

Examples:

- real products;
- architecture;
- facilities;
- people;
- completed work;
- processes;
- documented outcomes.

## 2.2 Explanation

Examples:

- diagrams;
- charts;
- maps;
- technical drawings;
- annotations;
- process graphics.

## 2.3 Navigation

Examples:

- icons;
- arrows;
- status symbols;
- category markers;
- wayfinding.

## 2.4 Atmosphere

Examples:

- abstract geometry;
- patterns;
- texture;
- background shapes;
- decorative motion.

Priority:

```text
Evidence > explanation > navigation > atmosphere
```

Atmosphere should never make evidence or explanation harder to read.

---

## 3. Visual direction

Swiss-inspired visual direction commonly uses:

- objective photography;
- strong geometric composition;
- precise cropping;
- monochrome or restrained color;
- clear captions;
- simplified pictograms;
- rational diagrams;
- modular image fields;
- rules and alignment lines;
- typographic graphics;
- limited decorative geometry.

The image system should feel related to the grid and typography.

---

## 4. Photography principles

## 4.1 Photography as information

A photograph should answer at least one question:

- What is this?
- Who is involved?
- Where does it happen?
- How does it work?
- What is the scale?
- What is the result?
- Why does it matter?

Avoid images that communicate only a vague mood.

## 4.2 Objectivity

Objective photography does not mean emotionally empty photography.

It means:

- accurate context;
- credible subjects;
- restrained staging;
- clear focal point;
- honest representation;
- appropriate captioning.

## 4.3 Composition

Preferred characteristics:

- strong horizontal and vertical lines;
- geometric relationships;
- clear negative space;
- intentional scale;
- readable foreground/background separation;
- controlled perspective;
- alignment opportunities.

## 4.4 Series rather than isolated images

For a project, case study, or feature, create:

1. establishing image;
2. medium context;
3. detail;
4. human interaction;
5. object or material;
6. outcome or finished state.

A coherent sequence is stronger than unrelated stock images.

---

## 5. Photography categories

## 5.1 Architecture

Use:

- frontal elevations;
- structural details;
- spatial sequence;
- material relationships;
- human scale;
- site context.

Avoid extreme perspective correction that makes the building feel unnatural.

## 5.2 Industrial and technical

Show:

- machinery;
- process;
- scale;
- precision;
- environment;
- safety;
- material;
- human operation.

Do not use technical imagery merely because it looks impressive.

## 5.3 Portraits

Swiss-inspired portraits may use:

- neutral or architectural background;
- direct gaze;
- controlled light;
- simple crop;
- environmental context;
- monochrome treatment.

Define one portrait system:

```text
Camera height
Lens character
Background
Lighting
Crop
Expression
Wardrobe
Color treatment
Aspect ratio
```

## 5.4 Product and object photography

Use:

- neutral view;
- contextual view;
- detail;
- scale reference;
- optional annotated view.

Preserve accurate:

- color;
- proportion;
- finish;
- included components;
- condition.

## 5.5 Documentary photography

Documentary images should include:

- date;
- location;
- credit;
- context;
- truthful description.

Do not use generated imagery as documentary proof.

---

## 6. Image treatment

## 6.1 Natural color

Use when accurate color is important.

Characteristics:

- neutral white balance;
- controlled saturation;
- realistic skin tones;
- visible material detail;
- balanced contrast.

## 6.2 Monochrome

Use for:

- source consistency;
- archival connection;
- typographic emphasis;
- visual reduction;
- documentary tone.

Rules:

- preserve tonal separation;
- retain facial detail;
- avoid crushed shadows;
- do not use monochrome to hide weak imagery.

## 6.3 Duotone or spot-color treatment

Suitable for:

- campaign sections;
- category systems;
- archival imagery;
- strong identity moments.

Use one consistent mapping.

Do not apply a different duotone to every card without a category system.

## 6.4 High contrast

High contrast can create strong graphic integration, but should not destroy evidence.

Use carefully for:

- poster-like hero imagery;
- graphic portraits;
- texture;
- secondary editorial images.

## 6.5 Grain and texture

Swiss Style normally favors clarity. If grain is used:

- keep it subtle;
- apply consistently;
- do not reduce text or subject clarity;
- avoid fake analog damage;
- optimize file size.

---

## 7. Cropping

## 7.1 Grid alignment

Image edges should frequently align with:

- columns;
- baseline sections;
- rules;
- text blocks;
- captions;
- page edges.

## 7.2 Crop types

Approved crop types:

- full bleed;
- column-aligned rectangle;
- extreme detail;
- frontal crop;
- geometric negative-space crop;
- modular image sequence.

## 7.3 Aspect-ratio system

Example:

```text
Hero: 16:9 or 3:2
Feature: 4:3 or 3:2
Card: 4:3
Portrait: 4:5 or 3:4
Square utility: 1:1
Panoramic: 2:1
```

Use a limited set.

## 7.4 Mobile cropping

Prepare a separate mobile crop when necessary.

Mobile crops should preserve:

- focal subject;
- product identity;
- face;
- relevant context;
- text-safe area.

Do not simply center-crop every desktop image.

---

## 8. Captions and credits

Captions are part of the design system.

A caption may include:

```text
Image number
Description
Location
Date
Photographer
Source
Rights
```

Example:

```text
03 — Assembly hall, Bergen, 2026
Photography: Name / Organization
```

Rules:

- Align captions to the grid.
- Use consistent typography.
- Place captions near the image.
- Distinguish caption from body text.
- Do not hide required credits in tooltips.
- Make factual captions specific.

---

## 9. Alternative text

Alternative text should describe the purpose of the image in context.

## 9.1 Informative image

Describe the useful content.

## 9.2 Functional image

Describe the action or destination.

## 9.3 Decorative image

Use empty alternative text:

```html
alt=""
```

## 9.4 Complex image

Provide:

- concise alt text;
- nearby detailed description;
- table or structured equivalent where appropriate.

Do not repeat the visible caption word for word unless it is the best equivalent.

---

## 10. Image and text relationships

Preferred relationships:

- image plus narrow caption column;
- full-width image plus indexed metadata;
- image spanning several grid columns;
- text aligned to image edge;
- image sequence with shared baseline;
- image and headline separated by clear negative space.

Avoid:

- arbitrary floating images;
- decorative overlap that obscures content;
- text over busy images;
- inconsistent image margins;
- random rounded image masks.

---

## 11. Illustration

Swiss-inspired illustration should be:

- reductive;
- geometric;
- diagrammatic;
- precise;
- conceptually clear;
- limited in color;
- integrated with typography.

Suitable approaches:

- geometric vector;
- line illustration;
- isometric technical view;
- modular pictorial system;
- abstract data form;
- simplified silhouette;
- collage with strong geometry.

Avoid generic character illustration unless the concept requires it.

---

## 12. Technical illustration

Technical illustration may include:

- exploded views;
- cutaways;
- process diagrams;
- component relationships;
- dimensional drawings;
- sectional views;
- assembly sequence.

Define:

```text
Stroke weight
Annotation style
Arrow style
Numbering
Scale behavior
Color coding
Legend
Export size
```

Technical clarity must take priority over visual styling.

---

## 13. Icon system

## 13.1 Icon grid

Choose one:

```text
16 × 16
20 × 20
24 × 24
32 × 32
```

A 24×24 grid is a practical default.

## 13.2 Icon anatomy

Define:

- stroke width;
- cap;
- join;
- corner radius;
- fill;
- optical alignment;
- safe area;
- minimum size.

Example:

```text
Grid: 24 × 24
Stroke: 1.5
Caps: square
Joins: miter or controlled round
Style: outline
```

## 13.3 Functional icons

Use for familiar actions:

- search;
- menu;
- close;
- download;
- external link;
- filter;
- sort;
- expand;
- previous/next.

Unfamiliar actions need text labels.

## 13.4 Service icons

Service icons should be:

- specific;
- simple;
- distinguishable;
- based on a shared visual grammar.

Avoid generic metaphors such as:

- lightbulb for innovation;
- rocket for growth;
- handshake for partnership;
- puzzle piece for solutions.

## 13.5 Icon containers

Prefer no container or a simple square system.

Avoid mixing:

- circles;
- pills;
- rounded squares;
- irregular blobs;
- outlined boxes;

without a documented rule.

---

## 14. Pictograms

Pictograms are especially appropriate for:

- transport;
- navigation;
- facilities;
- services;
- safety;
- public information;
- maps.

Requirements:

- clear silhouette;
- shared proportions;
- consistent perspective;
- cultural testing;
- text fallback where ambiguity exists;
- accessible naming.

Do not invent safety or regulatory pictograms.

---

## 15. Borders

## 15.1 Border roles

Borders may define:

- sections;
- cards;
- images;
- tables;
- inputs;
- selected state;
- emphasis.

## 15.2 Border weights

Example:

```css
--border-thin: 1px;
--border-strong: 2px;
```

Use a limited system.

## 15.3 Border rules

- Align to the grid.
- Use strong borders selectively.
- Do not frame every element.
- Ensure meaningful boundaries remain visible.
- Avoid hairlines that disappear on low-quality screens.
- Avoid ornamental border styles inconsistent with the rational system.

---

## 16. Separators and rules

Suitable separators:

- full-width horizontal rule;
- inset horizontal rule;
- vertical rule;
- short accent bar;
- numbered divider;
- label crossing a line;
- whitespace transition.

Rules:

- A separator must indicate grouping or sequence.
- Use whitespace for weak separation.
- Use lines for stronger separation.
- Align insets consistently.
- Do not place lines between every paragraph.
- Keep rule weights stable.

---

## 17. Arrows and connectors

Arrows may communicate:

- direction;
- continuation;
- sequence;
- route;
- dependency;
- relationship.

Define:

- shaft weight;
- arrowhead;
- corner behavior;
- spacing;
- direction convention.

Avoid decorative arrows pointing to nothing.

For diagrams:

- minimize crossing;
- label direction;
- preserve meaning without animation;
- create a mobile alternative.

---

## 18. Graphic geometry

Swiss graphic systems often use simple forms:

- square;
- rectangle;
- circle;
- line;
- bar;
- grid;
- coordinate;
- typographic mark.

Use geometry to:

- index;
- group;
- emphasize;
- measure;
- frame;
- indicate state;
- connect.

Do not use random geometry as filler.

---

## 19. Supergraphics

Supergraphics use oversized typography or geometry across large surfaces.

On the web, they may appear as:

- oversized numbers;
- giant cropped letters;
- full-width bars;
- large directional arrows;
- background typographic fragments;
- section indexes.

Rules:

- Keep content readable.
- Ensure they do not appear interactive.
- Prevent viewport overflow.
- Reduce or remove on compact screens where needed.
- Hide decorative forms appropriately from assistive technology.
- Do not make the entire site depend on oversized decoration.

---

## 20. Typographic graphics

Typography may become a graphic asset through:

- oversized numbers;
- coordinates;
- section codes;
- year markers;
- abbreviations;
- type-only posters;
- structured lists;
- repeated words;
- typographic diagrams.

Meaningful type should remain live HTML whenever possible.

Do not rasterize exact interface text.

---

## 21. Charts

Swiss principles suit precise data visualization.

Rules:

- choose the simplest correct chart;
- use clear axes;
- include units;
- use direct labels;
- avoid 3D;
- avoid decorative gradients;
- reserve accent color for emphasis;
- use neutral grid lines;
- provide source and update date;
- include accessible table or text equivalent.

Appropriate charts:

- bar;
- line;
- scatter;
- histogram;
- timeline;
- table;
- map when geography matters.

---

## 22. Diagrams

Suitable diagram types:

- process;
- architecture;
- system map;
- timeline;
- hierarchy;
- service model;
- route;
- comparison.

Rules:

- one main message;
- limited node types;
- consistent shapes;
- direct labels;
- predictable reading direction;
- minimal line crossings;
- strong mobile fallback;
- accessible textual equivalent.

---

## 23. Maps

Swiss-style maps should prioritize:

- relevant geography;
- clear labels;
- route hierarchy;
- restrained basemap detail;
- consistent symbols;
- accessible location list.

Avoid decorative world maps used only to imply global reach.

---

## 24. Patterns

Suitable patterns:

- modular grid;
- dot matrix;
- repeated line;
- coordinate field;
- geometric repeat;
- typographic repeat.

Rules:

- Keep patterns subordinate.
- Avoid reducing text contrast.
- Use CSS or SVG.
- Prevent visible seams.
- Do not apply pattern to every surface.
- Tie pattern scale to the grid.

---

## 25. Texture

Swiss visual systems generally use minimal texture.

Appropriate texture may include:

- subtle paper;
- print grain;
- photographic grain;
- architectural material;
- scan texture for archival content.

Texture should not:

- obscure text;
- simulate random aging;
- dominate the page;
- create unnecessary file weight.

---

## 26. Interface screenshots

For digital products:

- show real interface;
- crop to relevant workflow;
- use realistic data;
- remove sensitive content;
- preserve legibility;
- avoid extreme perspective distortion;
- label conceptual UI clearly;
- use annotations outside the screenshot when possible.

Avoid fictional floating dashboards.

---

## 27. Video

Use video for:

- process;
- movement;
- spatial experience;
- product behavior;
- interviews;
- documentary context.

Requirements:

- captions;
- poster image;
- no autoplay audio;
- pause controls where required;
- reduced-motion alternative;
- optimized formats;
- no essential text only in video.

---

## 28. Motion assets

Suitable motion:

- line drawing;
- grid reveal;
- image crop;
- number transition;
- directional slide;
- typographic width or weight change;
- diagram sequencing.

Avoid:

- bouncing;
- floating;
- elastic overshoot;
- excessive parallax;
- flashing;
- motion that delays content.

## 29. Responsive image delivery

Example:

```html
<picture>
  <source
    media="(max-width: 48rem)"
    type="image/avif"
    srcset="
      /images/project-mobile-640.avif 640w,
      /images/project-mobile-960.avif 960w
    "
    sizes="100vw"
  />
  <source
    type="image/avif"
    srcset="
      /images/project-960.avif 960w,
      /images/project-1440.avif 1440w,
      /images/project-1920.avif 1920w
    "
    sizes="(min-width: 90rem) 90rem, 100vw"
  />
  <img
    src="/images/project-1440.webp"
    width="1920"
    height="1280"
    alt="Descriptive alternative text"
    loading="lazy"
    decoding="async"
  />
</picture>
```

Rules:

- define intrinsic dimensions;
- use responsive widths;
- prioritize the LCP image;
- lazy-load below-fold images;
- use art-directed crops;
- use AVIF or WebP for photography;
- use SVG for diagrams and icons;
- test compression of line detail.

---

## 30. File formats

### AVIF

Use for:

- photography;
- complex imagery;
- high compression efficiency.

### WebP

Use for:

- broad modern support;
- photography;
- transparent raster assets.

### JPEG

Use for fallback photography where required.

### PNG

Use for:

- crisp transparent raster;
- source screenshots;
- pixel-accurate assets where lossy compression is harmful.

### SVG

Use for:

- icons;
- diagrams;
- maps;
- line illustration;
- patterns;
- logos;
- borders;
- separators.

Do not embed large photographs inside SVG.

## 31. Asset naming

Use descriptive names.

```text
hero-architecture-grid-wide.avif
hero-architecture-grid-mobile.avif
portrait-director-neutral-4x5.webp
project-facade-detail-03.webp
icon-service-engineering.svg
diagram-process-sequence.svg
separator-indexed-rule.svg
pattern-modular-grid.svg
```

Avoid:

```text
final2.jpg
image-new.png
asset123.svg
```

## 32. Rights and provenance

Record:

- creator;
- source;
- date;
- location;
- license;
- consent;
- modifications;
- caption;
- credit;
- approved use;
- expiry or review date.

Never imply that generated or stock imagery depicts:

- actual employees;
- actual customers;
- actual facilities;
- completed work;
- documentary events.

## 33. AI image-generation instructions

A prompt should define:

1. objective visual direction;
2. subject;
3. setting;
4. real activity;
5. composition;
6. geometry;
7. lighting;
8. palette;
9. page role;
10. negative-space direction;
11. aspect ratio;
12. excluded clichés.

Example:

```text
Objective contemporary Swiss-style architectural photograph.
Subject: a modern public building with visible structural grid and pedestrians
providing scale. Frontal composition, precise vertical lines, neutral daylight,
restrained natural color, clear negative space in the upper-left for HTML text.
No dramatic cinematic haze, no fake signage, no text, no exaggerated wide-angle
distortion, no decorative gradients. Aspect ratio 3:2.
```

Generated imagery must not be presented as documentary evidence.

---

## 34. Anti-patterns

Avoid:

- generic monochrome imagery unrelated to content;
- applying red overlays to every photo;
- random geometric crops;
- inconsistent caption placement;
- mixing several icon families;
- excessive rounded image masks;
- fake technical diagrams;
- decorative world maps;
- charts without source or unit;
- arrows that do not communicate direction;
- grid overlays used as wallpaper;
- text baked into images;
- low-resolution raster diagrams;
- unlicensed logos;
- generated images presented as real projects;
- one image preset for every asset role.

---

## 35. AI-agent instructions

An AI agent building Swiss-style visual assets must:

1. classify each asset as evidence, explanation, navigation, or atmosphere;
2. define the primary visual direction;
3. align image composition with the grid;
4. define a limited aspect-ratio system;
5. define caption and credit format;
6. define icon grid and stroke;
7. define border and separator rules;
8. define chart and diagram style;
9. preserve factual accuracy;
10. prepare mobile crops;
11. optimize all formats;
12. recommend alt text;
13. record rights and provenance;
14. avoid false documentary representation;
15. keep decorative geometry subordinate.

## 38. Final rule

Swiss-style visual assets must clarify before they decorate. Photography provides evidence, diagrams explain systems, icons guide, borders organize, and geometry creates hierarchy. Every asset should belong to the same rational visual language.
