# Product Illustration System 2026

> Rules for illustrations, pictograms, characters, empty-state graphics, onboarding visuals, diagrams, and explanatory artwork inside authenticated products.

## 1. Purpose

Product illustration should help users understand, orient, learn, recover, or feel supported.

It should not be added simply to fill empty space.

## 2. Illustration roles

Use illustration for:

- onboarding;
- empty states;
- setup;
- education;
- feature explanation;
- system concepts;
- errors requiring calm explanation;
- product identity;
- achievements used carefully.

## 3. Productive versus expressive

Carbon distinguishes productive pictograms, which work across many product contexts, from more expressive artwork.

Use productive graphics for explanation and repeated UI. Reserve expressive illustration for selected moments.

## 4. Empty states

Carbon describes empty states as opportunities to explain why data is absent and guide users toward a productive next step.

Illustration is optional. The heading, explanation, and action remain primary.

## 5. Illustration system

Define:

```text
stroke
fill
palette
perspective
depth
texture
corner style
human proportions
facial detail
object accuracy
background complexity
motion behavior
```

## 6. Scale tiers

Create separate detail levels for:

- icon-sized symbol;
- pictogram;
- component illustration;
- page illustration;
- onboarding scene.

Do not shrink a complex illustration to icon size.

## 7. Characters and people

Represent people with dignity, agency, and context. Avoid stereotypes, tokenism, and generic “diverse team” scenes.

Do not use generated people as real users or customers.

## 8. Technical accuracy

Product, accessibility, safety, and domain objects require expert review.

Do not draw an assistive device, regulated symbol, industrial process, or technical system inaccurately.

## 9. Color

Use semantic color carefully. Illustration colors must not conflict with product status colors.

Verify contrast where illustration communicates meaning.

## 10. Motion

Animated illustration may explain sequence or feedback. Provide pause and reduced-motion alternatives for repeating or significant motion.

## 11. Accessibility

Decorative illustration uses empty alt text. Informative illustration needs purpose-focused alt text. Complex explanatory graphics need detailed text.

Do not embed critical exact instructions only inside artwork.

## 12. Dark mode

Prepare theme-aware palettes or use surfaces that protect illustration legibility. Do not automatically invert artwork.

## 13. Localization

Avoid culturally specific gestures, metaphors, clothing, symbols, or text unless intended and reviewed.

## 14. Asset formats

Use SVG for scalable line and flat graphics, raster for painterly texture, and video/Lottie only where motion is justified and accessible.

## 15. Governance

Record creator, license, source, usage, status, version, and AI involvement.

## 16. Anti-patterns

Avoid illustration in every empty state, mascots hiding serious errors, fake diversity, inaccurate accessibility symbols, decorative charts, and generated lettering.

## 17. Agent rules

An AI agent must identify illustration purpose, choose the correct scale tier, preserve content hierarchy, define accessibility, and avoid illustration when clear text or a real product example is better.

## 18. Checklist

- [ ] Clear role
- [ ] Productive/expressive tier
- [ ] Style system
- [ ] Scale variants
- [ ] Representation review
- [ ] Technical accuracy
- [ ] Dark mode
- [ ] Reduced motion
- [ ] Alt-text decision
- [ ] Rights and AI status
